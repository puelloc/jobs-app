## The headline: the largest bucket is not a browser-use problem

`no_homepage` is **562 of 866 unresolved (65%)** and it is entirely a tier-1 failure. Every one of
the 562 has `website IS NULL`, and **all 562 had no `wikipedia_infobox` attempt at all** - the count
of infobox attempts (936) exactly equals the count of non-NULL websites.

That equality is the finding: an infobox attempt exists only when a homepage was found, so the 562
produced no attempt and no homepage. They split into two causes:

1. **Companies with no Wikipedia article.** The parser records which rows have an article, and the
   plan puts those ~252 rows in the SEC 10-K tier - which is deferred. Expected, and unfixable
   within the current tiers.
2. **Companies whose article exists.** Sampled: `advanced-micro-devices` (article "AMD"),
   `advance-auto-parts-inc`, `a10-networks-inc`. Verified by hand that these titles resolve fine
   against `action=query` and that a real 50-title batch returns 200 with 50 pages. So the batch
   mechanism works and these are being lost somewhere between the request and the result map.
   **Not diagnosed.** This is the highest-value open item: it is ~310 companies and it costs no
   network round trips to fix.

## §8.1 call: browser-use is not justified

The §8.1 threshold is the **blocked** residue: **148 companies, 9.9%**, which lands in the
"judgment call" 5-20% band. Two things settle it against browser-use as the next step:

1. **The blocked set is diffuse, not concentrated.** The largest single contributor is
   `ferguson.com` with 4 rows; the rest are 1-2 each across many unrelated hosts. A concentrated set
   would suggest one targeted fix (a required UA header, one ATS host). A diffuse set of 403s is
   what a real browser would have to solve one site at a time.
2. **It is not the largest bucket by a wide margin.** At 9.9% against the 37.5% no-homepage residue,
   browser-use would address the third-largest problem while the largest one is a join failure that
   no browser can help with.

**Recommendation: fix tier 1 first, then re-measure.** If the blocked share holds near 10% after the
no-homepage bucket is repaired, browser-use is a judgment call on ~150 companies - worth a spike,
not worth making the primary M3 driver. The 6.3% policy-bug bucket should be fixed regardless: those
are companies the gate refuses for reasons that are wrong, not sites that refuse us.

## Two bugs found in the run's data

**1. Sitemap declarations recorded as accepted attempts (fixed, `958c0a7`).** 2,957 of 6,348 attempt
rows - **46.6%** - were `robots_sitemap` entries with an empty `final_url`: inputs the tier uses to
build candidates, recorded as though they were candidates. Salesforce alone contributed 53. The rows
inflated the trail and broke the query the table exists to answer. The existing run keeps its noise
because the rows are written; this analysis excludes them explicitly.

**2. The `no_homepage` bucket above**, which is the real remaining work.

## Method notes

- The category table is computed **per company, not per attempt**. An attempt-level count is
  misleading here: of 6,348 rows, 2,957 were the sitemap noise and most accepted rows are homepage
  fetches rather than careers finds.
- Buckets are assigned by priority, so a company appears once. `blocked` is checked before `policy`
  because a 403 on one candidate and a policy rejection on another is a blocked site, not a bug.
- Spot-checks were run for the two large buckets before the labels were trusted. The `no_homepage`
  check is what surfaced finding 2.
